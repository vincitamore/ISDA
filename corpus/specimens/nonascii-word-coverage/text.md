# Loanwords keep their accents longer than their meanings

English borrows faster than it naturalises. A café stays a café for a century after the
naïveté of calling it a coffee-house has passed; the jalapeño keeps its tilde long after
the menu forgets where the pepper came from. The façade of a borrowed word is often the
last part to erode — résumé, cliché, entrée and protégé all still carry marks their
neighbours abandoned, and a crème brûlée without its circumflex reads like a recipe
typed in a hurry.

The pattern is older than the printing press. Greek gave us λόγος and τέχνη, ἀρετή and
καιρός, and the words survived transliteration even when the concepts arrived bruised.
Russian sent спутник around the planet in a single autumn, then перестройка a generation
later, and both kept their syllable weight when the alphabet changed underneath them. A
smörgåsbord is not quite a buffet, a fjord is not quite a bay, and Göttingen is not
quite pronounceable the way its spelling promises.

Food carries diacritics further than philosophy does. The phở arrives with its hook and
tone marks intact because the soup is not translatable; bánh mì keeps both accents on a
menu that renders everything else in plain type. The pączki sold the Tuesday before Lent
keep their ogonek in cities that cannot say Łódź; açaí keeps its cedilla and its acute
through every smoothie stand from São Paulo northward; şiş arrives skewered exactly as
İstanbul spells it, dotted capital and all.

None of this is decoration. A señora is not a senora, an über-cautious editor is not an
uber-cautious one, and the garçon who becomes a garcon has lost his profession's
history. The accent is the word's passport — proof it came from somewhere, and proof
the borrowing language agreed, for a while, to remember where.
