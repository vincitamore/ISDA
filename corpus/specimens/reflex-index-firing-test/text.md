# Bench Notes Index

> Sparse by design. Procedure detail lives in the linked runbooks; this index holds only
> what must fire before a lookup would happen. One line per entry, admission only after a
> mistake has actually occurred, removal the moment a fixture makes an entry obsolete.

- [Water before wiring](runbooks/water-before-wiring.md) — the misting loop pressurises on
  power-up, so any valve work done live sprays the terminal block; kill pressure at the
  manifold FIRST, then open the controller cabinet, and never trust the panel gauge alone
  because it reads the pump side, not the line you are about to open
- [The rack has two north ends](runbooks/rack-orientation.md) — tray coordinates are
  written from the door side but the controller numbers rows from the window side, so a
  hand-moved tray logged without converting is silently swapped with its mirror; convert
  at the whiteboard, never in your head, and re-count from the door after every move
- [Seed packets lie about days-to-sprout](runbooks/germination-windows.md) — printed
  windows assume bottom heat we do not have, so add four days to every packet figure
  before planning a rotation; the one exception is basil, which beats its packet by two
  days and will outgrow its cell if you believe the label
- [The moisture sensor drifts wet](runbooks/sensor-calibration.md) — after three weeks
  uncalibrated it reads a dry tray as damp, which cancels watering exactly when watering
  matters; calibrate against the reference brick on the first of the month and log the
  offset, because an unlogged calibration is indistinguishable from a skipped one
- [Never top-water the covered trays](runbooks/watering-modes.md) — condensation under a
  dome recycles moisture for days, and adding more breeds mould at the stem line; if the
  dome fogs, vent it, and if the mat under an uncovered tray is dry, water the MAT, not
  the soil surface
- [Label the tray, not the shelf](runbooks/labelling.md) — shelves get rearranged during
  cleaning and a shelf label follows the wood, not the plants; the tray edge is the only
  surface that travels with what it names, and a marker that survives misting is the only
  marker that counts
